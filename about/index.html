About

Hi, I'm Alistair.

An educator with psychology training, and the person behind Neurodiverse Aotearoa.

I never planned to go into education. I got a job teaching swimming at a university, and realised quickly that I loved it and I was good at working with kids. I'd done a lot of sports coaching at high school, and it had that same feel. So I trained as a teacher.

I qualified at a time when there were more teachers than jobs, so I did relief work for a while. A family friend who taught dyslexia using the Davis method asked if I'd like to do some private tutoring, and that's where things really started to click. Working alongside her, I realised I was dyslexic myself, which explained a lot about my own time at school.

ADHD came a little later. At first I thought, no, that's not me, because I could focus really well when I was interested in something. It turns out there's a lot more to ADHD than that. I'm also lucky to share my life with a partner who is autistic, so this work is personal.

From the classroom to the whole school

Back in the classroom, I found I worked really well with students who sat a little outside the usual boundaries. I became fascinated with research and with refining my teaching, and my classroom practice grew quite innovative. But I kept noticing that children lost that consistency when they moved between classes, and I realised I couldn't fix that from inside one room. So I became a primary school principal, to see if we could make change on a bigger scale.

Over about ten years in learning support, I've taught in primary, secondary and university settings.

Every family's situation is different, so I start by listening. Some parents have spent years feeling unheard or even blamed, and many children have started to believe something is wrong with them. Being heard and understood is where change begins.

Away from work

I like being busy. I'm into cooking and gardening, I enjoy woodworking, especially furniture making, and I've just started bookbinding. What ties it together is that I love learning new things, which is a handy habit when you're helping people who learn differently.
